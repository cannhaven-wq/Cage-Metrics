In plain English
  • We collect as little as we can. We don't sell your data and we run no advertising pixels.
  • We count visits with Plausible Analytics, which sets no cookies, and with our own anonymous counter. Neither stores your name, email or account.
  • CFL itself sets no cookies.
  • If you make an account, we hold your email and what you save — My Book bets, starred fights, alerts. Only your account can see them.
  • We name every company that helps us run the site, below.
  • You can ask us to show you, correct or delete your data through the contact page.

2. What we collect, and why

Category by category

When you visit any page

We count visits. We don't build a profile of you.

Plausible Analytics. Plausible is a third-party analytics service that processes visit data on our behalf. It runs on every public page of the site except password reset, unsubscribe, a handful of pages that only redirect elsewhere, and one unlinked internal page. It records which page was viewed, the referring site, your browser and device type, and your approximate location (country, region and city, worked out from your IP address), and it counts unique visitors. We send Plausible the name of a page action (for example "factor_lab_view") and nothing else — no extra details about you or what you clicked. According to Plausible, it sets no cookies, does not follow you to other websites, and does not store your IP address. [LAWYER: verify the last sentence against Plausible's current Data Processing Agreement before publishing — it is Plausible's claim, not one we can check.]

Our own anonymous counter. We also keep our own record of which pages and features are used, so we can tell which parts of the site are worth building on. Each record holds the name of the action (for example "a fight was opened"), the time, and a few details about it:

  • the page path, the website you came from (the site name only, not the full address) and any campaign tag in the link;
  • which fight, event or sort order was involved, where that is the point of the action;
  • whether your browser reports that it is being driven by a script, or names itself as a search-engine crawler — so we can count people rather than robots;
  • a random session code, stored in your browser's session storage. It is new every visit, disappears when you close the tab, and is never linked to your account.

These records never contain your email address, your name, your account identifier, your IP address, a device fingerprint, any cross-site identifier, or any bet amount or bankroll figure. This is enforced in our code, which strips those fields before anything is sent, and in the database, which limits what a record can hold. Nobody outside CFL can read these records, and we look at them only as daily totals. [LAWYER: is "anonymous" the right legal word here, or must it be "pseudonymised" because a per-session code exists? The technical facts are as stated; the label is a legal call.]

If you create an account

Your email, and what you choose to save.

  • Sign-in details. Your email address and password. Passwords are handled by our sign-in provider, Supabase, and stored only in scrambled (hashed) form; we never see them.
  • Account settings. Your membership status (free, beta or paid) and your email-alert preferences.
  • My Book. The bets and bankroll figures you enter. The database lets only your account read or change them. They are never used for analytics or marketing.
  • Watchlist and alerts. The fights you star, the alerts you set, and a record of each alert email we send you — kept so the same alert is never sent twice.

We use this to run your account and the features you use, and for nothing else. [LAWYER: people who operate CFL can technically reach the database for maintenance. Confirm whether the policy must say so, and in what words.]

If you sign up for the Cannon Card Brief

We store your email address, where on the site you signed up, when, and a private unsubscribe code. We are building a confirmation step: you will get one email asking you to confirm the address, and we will not send the Card Brief to an address until it has been confirmed. Until that step is finished, the Card Brief is not being sent to anyone — this is locked in our code, not just a promise. Every Brief will carry a one-click unsubscribe link.

If you contact us

The contact form collects your name, your email and your message so we can reply. It is delivered to us by Formspree, a form-handling service.

If you pay us (not active)

CFL takes no payments today. If a paid plan launches, payment will be handled by Stripe. We will never see or store your card details. We will store a Stripe customer reference, a subscription reference, the subscription's status and the date your paid period ends, plus a log of the billing messages Stripe sends us, which no member and no browser can read.

Stored in your browser

CFL sets no cookies. A few things are kept in your browser's own storage.

  • If you sign in: your sign-in session, so you stay signed in (local storage).
  • The date of your last visit, used only to count how many days pass between visits. It stays in your browser; only the number of days is ever counted (local storage).
  • The random session code described above, and a note of whether this visit has already been counted (session storage, cleared when you close the tab).

You can clear all of it in your browser settings at any time. [LAWYER: do EU/UK rules on storing information on a device (ePrivacy) require consent for any of these, given none is a cookie? The sign-in session is strictly necessary; the last-visit date and session code are for analytics.]

4. What we don't do

Plain commitments
  • We do not sell your personal information, and we do not share it for cross-site advertising.
  • We run no advertising pixels and no cross-site trackers.
  • CFL carries no affiliate links today, so no sportsbook receives anything about you from us.
  • We do not use your data to train artificial-intelligence models.

7. Security

How we protect it

Saved data is protected in the database so that each account can reach only its own rows, and anonymous visitors cannot read anyone's data. No system is perfectly secure, and we cannot promise absolute security. If a breach affects your personal data, we will tell you as the law requires.
