# T-082 — Does the live engine compute what its backtest computed?

**Date:** 2026-10-12 · **Author:** Claude · **Status:** done. Read-only and internal.
Nothing here goes on a public surface (D-011).

**Evidence:** [`diagnose_live_path.py`](diagnose_live_path.py), run read-only in
GitHub Actions ([run 38298613704](https://github.com/cannhaven-wq/Cage-Metrics/actions/runs/38298613704)),
with every number copied into [`results_2026-10-12.json`](results_2026-10-12.json).
Live-database figures come from read-only SQL in this session.

## The answer, plainly

**Yes. The live engine does what its backtest did.** It reads the same inputs,
builds the same features and produces the same kind of forecast. No bug in the
live pipeline explains the bad live record.

**What does explain it:** the engine is normally worse than the betting market,
and these eight cards were hard for everyone. The market itself scored 0.645,
against its usual 0.600. Measured against the market, the engine's live
shortfall is the kind of gap one run of eight cards in four produces in the
development years. It is not an outlier.

**The live record is still a fair record.** The pre-fight snapshots match the
published picks exactly. Nothing needs restating or withdrawing.

**One real defect turned up, and it matters for any future experiment.** The
published live numbers **cannot be reproduced exactly**. Re-running the same
code on the same days gives the same overall picture, but individual fights
move by 4.5 points on average and up to 38 points. 1 in 10 flips to the other
fighter. Any forecast a future experiment locks must therefore be a frozen,
hashed file, never a model retrained on the morning (T-083).

## The four leads the brief named, and what became of each

1. **Corner orientation.** The strongest early signal. Live picks back the A
   corner 41% of the time, while the market favours A in 65% of these fights.
   Nearly every live disagreement with the market goes the same way: the engine
   takes B and the market takes A (18 of 22).
   **Not a bug.** The same tilt is present when the backtest code runs on
   2022–24: the engine picks A 44% while the market favours A 61%, and
   disagreements go 139 to 37 in the same direction. The engine systematically
   underrates the fighter the market favours, who is usually in the A corner.
   That is a fact about what the engine knows, not a broken pipe.
   *Not checked here, and worth knowing:* the published backtest's yearly A
   share (51–58%) is higher than this retraining's 44%. The tilt's size moves
   with each retraining, but its direction does not.
2. **Feature freshness.** Live picks lock a median of 11.3 days before the
   bout. That costs nothing measurable: the serving features equal the backtest
   features on every fight that could be compared.
3. **Calibration map.** The live path applies one calibration fitted to the
   pooled walk-forward output, where the backtest refits it fold by fold. On
   2022–24 the difference is inside seed noise. Live probabilities are more
   confident (26% at 70% or above, against 7% in the backtest), but the market
   was also more confident about these cards (it favoured someone at 67.9% on
   average, against 65.2%). The reproduced numbers show the same 24%.
4. **Something else in the live code.** Nothing found.

## Smaller findings

- **21 of the 98 scored fights are engine_v1 picks from 18 July**, locked 35–49
  days before the bout. The July runbook said those rows should be cleared as
  stale (`cleanup_stale_live_picks.sql`). They were not, so the snapshotter
  froze them. That is the honest record of what was shown, and it stays. Split
  by version: engine_v1 0.666 (21 fights) and engine_v2 0.731 (77). The market
  scored 0.679 and 0.636 on the same fights.
- **`wc_code` is not stable between builds.** Weight class reaches the model as
  an integer assigned in order of first appearance. A label first seen on an
  upcoming card, such as a Road to UFC or TUF tournament bracket, gets a code
  the model never trained on, and the same fight gets different codes in
  different builds. It affected 9 of 102 fights and is small. It belongs to
  T-083.
- **The production dependencies float.** `cfl_engine/requirements.txt` is all
  `>=`. The diagnostic ran with whatever the runner installed that day, as
  production does. That is the most likely half of the reproduction gap. The
  other half is history corrected after publish. **Neither cause was isolated
  here;** T-083 does that.

## Sealed-data statement (PRED-001)

Check C read only fights dated before 2025-01-01, and asserts it. Checks A and
B re-ran the incumbent engine the way production already runs it every
morning: trained on all completed fights. They compared probabilities and
features only, scored nothing on sealed data and printed no sealed outcome.
The block resampling used walk-forward rows dated before 2025-01-01 only. No
H1 slot was spent.

## What this changes

- **T-082 is closed.** The owner's Priority 1 question has an answer: the
  system produces the predictions its research says it should. Its research
  also says those predictions lose to the market.
- **PRED-002 may proceed** to its freeze and Stage 1 (D-023). Nothing here
  touches its design: it never reads the live engine.
- **New: T-083.** Make every live forecast reproducible: a saved model file
  and its hash per publish, pinned dependencies, and a stable weight-class
  encoding. Required before any prospective lock is trusted.
