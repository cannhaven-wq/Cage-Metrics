## Plain verdict

**Both projects are sound, they agree, and neither found anything.** Not one of
the 20 + 660 ideas they tested made the closing odds better at picking
winners. Where they test the same factor, they get the same answer.

**The one place they seemed to disagree is settled:** the engine's "the
market was a little under-confident in 2019–24" is mostly a side effect of
how the bookmaker's margin was removed. Use a different, equally standard
method and it shrinks from +0.0023 to +0.0002 (power) or +0.0008 (Shin). It
is not a betting edge either way.

**Recommendation:** make the Discovery Engine the one research framework, keep
DISC-001 as its independent cross-check, fix two engine defects before its
next run, and **do not run another broad search**. The next useful work is in
[`PHASE2_RESEARCH_DESIGN_2026-10-21.md`](PHASE2_RESEARCH_DESIGN_2026-10-21.md).

**Two things need the owner before the integration merges**: whether raw data
exports may sit on `main` in a public repository (§6), and approval of the
consolidation itself (L3 item 4). The full list is in §8.

## 4. Reconciliation

### 4.1 The one conflict: is the closing price under-confident?

DISC-001 says the gain from re-weighting the market is an artefact of the
proportional de-vig. The engine reports it as the 2019–24 close being
under-confident (slope 1.23), "the largest effect seen", and lists it as a
direction for more research. Recomputed on both windows, all three methods
(gain from re-weighting the market's own number, per fight, 95% card range):

| window | proportional | power | Shin |
|---|---|---|---|
| DISC-001, 2012–24 (5,792) | +0.0012 (+0.0002 to +0.0021) | −0.0001 (−0.0005 to +0.0003) | +0.0003 (−0.0004 to +0.0009) |
| engine screen, 2010–18 (3,509) | +0.0002 (−0.0012 to +0.0016) | −0.0003 (−0.0010 to +0.0004) | −0.0002 (−0.0011 to +0.0007) |
| engine confirm, 2019–24 (2,826) | **+0.0023** (+0.0011 to +0.0034) | +0.0002 (−0.0000 to +0.0005) | +0.0008 (+0.0002 to +0.0014) |

**Reading, without choosing a side:**

- Both projects computed their own numbers correctly.
- Most of the effect is the de-vig: the BFO closing consensus carries a high
  margin (median 7.7% in 2020–24, BT-001), and proportional removal leaves
  long shots too likely. Under power the 2019–24 slope is 1.06, not 1.23.
- **DISC-001's "vanishes" is slightly too strong for 2019–24 alone:** under
  Shin a small gain (+0.0008) survives there. **The engine's "under-confident"
  is too strong as stated:** it depends on the de-vig choice, and the engine's
  report never checked that. Under every method it is below PRED-001's 0.003
  bar, and it concerns a consensus price nobody could bet (BT-001).
- **Consequence for every future PRED-001 experiment.** The protocol's primary
  benchmark is the proportional de-vig. A blend that does nothing but re-weight
  the market collects about +0.001 (2012–24) to +0.002 (2019–24) against it for
  free — up to three-quarters of the 0.003 bar in recent years. PRED-001 §4
  already reports power and Shin as sensitivities and forbids choosing between
  them after the fact, so the protocol is not changed here. The recommendation
  is that every preregistration also reports a "re-weighted market only"
  model, so this free gain is visible beside any claimed one.

| factor (DISC-001 / engine) | DISC-001 2012–18 | engine 2010–18 | DISC-001 2019–24 | engine 2019–24 | both say |

| age (F13 / `d_age`) | +0.62 | +0.32 | +0.93 | +0.14 | nothing measurable |
| UFC win rate (F14 / `d_ufc_raw_rate`) | +0.26 | +0.44 | +0.24 | +0.11 | nothing measurable |

The definitions differ in detail (DISC-001 smooths win rate and leaves age
undefined for a debut; the engine does neither), and the answers still line
up. Every range in both projects crosses zero. **No contradiction.**

### 4.3 Where a result could be misread as "disproven"

- **Rare factors were not disproven; they were barely testable.** DISC-001's
  own positive control finds a planted 55% effect on "coming off a KO loss"
  0 times in 40, and a 57% effect 2 times in 40. Its table still labels F02,
  F03, F04, F08 and F09 "adds nothing measurable", while the engine labels the
  same idea "too few fights to judge". Read both as **"not detected; the test
  could only have seen a large effect."** DISC-001's report says so in its
  sensitivity section; the table label is stronger than its own evidence.
- **The engine's handoff line "the factors in the database add nothing to the
  closing price"** overreaches: 75 of its 660 ideas were too sparse to judge,
  and every idea was tested in one linear form.
- **Untested is not tested.** Neither project looked at round-by-round data,
  opponent-adjusted measures, injuries, travel, short-notice replacements,
  missed weight or anything about when prices move. Both reports list these;
  any summary of "factors don't work" must carry the same list.
- **Age and UFC record cannot become historical evidence for anything.** They
  were chosen because of a Factor Lab verdict computed over all years
  including the sealed period (PRED-001 §2). DISC-001 flags this; the engine
  does not (§5, E3).

## 5. Engine audit — defects to fix before its next run

None of these changed a Phase 1 result: the shortlist was empty, so no
candidate reached the code where they live.

| id | defect | effect | fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| **E1** | **The de-vig stability gate is silently skipped on real data.** The engine computes power and Shin with the frozen CLV-001 helpers, which refuse prices beyond 97%. 7 scorable DEV fights are (3 for Shin), so their power/Shin values are NaN, `run.py` skips the sensitivity for that method without recording it in `results.json`, and `sens_ok = all([])` is **True**. | a lead would pass "still positive under power and Shin" without being checked | evaluate the sensitivity on the fights where it is defined, or use band-free power/Shin for historical BFO prices as DISC-001 does; record which |
| **E2** | **Significance and size are judged against different references.** A single factor must beat the *re-weighted* market to pass the screen, but the 0.003 size test compares with the *raw* market, which in 2019–24 already includes the +0.0023 de-vig gain of §4.1. | a factor adding +0.0008 of its own could clear the 0.003 bar on borrowed credit | **owner decision** (two defensible paths, L3 item 10): PRED-001's own Δ is against the raw market. Recommended: require the size test against the re-weighted market for a factor lead and report both |
| **E3** | no H1-contamination flag on `d_age`, `d_ufc_raw_rate`, `d_ufc_net`, `d_win_rate`, `d_td_def` | a lead built on them could be mistaken for clean historical evidence | add the flag to the feature manifest, as DISC-001 does |
| E4 | the config and catalog were committed 33 seconds after the first real run started (`c112794d` 20:02:24 vs run `20261021T200151Z`) | "fixed before the first run" cannot be shown from git; the ledger's config and code hashes do match the committed files | commit the config before any future real run |

**Engine reproduction (ledger entry 1)** was run in a scratch copy (§3), not
appended to `ledger.jsonl`: an identical rerun asks nothing new, and a ledger
entry would read as a second look at 2019–24.

**The 2019–24 period is used.** Both projects have now looked at every DEV
year from 2010 to 2024, and the engine published 2019–24 numbers for all 660
ideas. DEV can still be used to *explore*, but it is no longer a clean place
to *confirm* anything either catalogue touched. Confirmation now means a
sealed-set look (10 ever, about 0.008 detectable) or a prospective test.
