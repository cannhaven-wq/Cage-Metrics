## 1. Inventory — what is actually in the database

### T-1. Depth and coverage

```sql
with f as (
  select f.id, e.event_date from fights f join events e on e.id=f.event_id
)
select (select count(*) from f) fights_total,
 (select count(*) from f where event_date < current_date) fights_past,
 (select min(event_date) from f) first_event,
 (select count(*) from fight_rounds) round_rows,
 (select count(distinct fight_id) from fight_rounds) fights_with_rounds,
 (select count(*) from fighters) fighters_total;
```

| measure | value |
|---|---|
| fights total | 9,006 |
| fights already fought | 8,944 |
| first event | 1994-03-11 |
| **round rows** | **20,905** |
| **fights with round detail** | **8,882** (99.3% of fought) |
| fighters | 4,549 |
| fought fights with no winner recorded | 193 |

`fight_rounds` carries, **per round and per corner**: knockdowns, significant
strikes landed and attempted, total strikes landed and attempted, takedowns
landed and attempted, submission attempts, reversals, control seconds, and the
head / body / leg and distance / clinch / ground breakdowns of strikes landed.

This is the asset. It is round-level, it is two-sided, and it is essentially
complete from 2000 onward.

### T-2. Two structural checks that had to pass

**Corner ordering is consistent.** If `fight_rounds.fighter_a_id` disagreed
with `fights.fighter_a_id` even occasionally, every per-round metric would
silently attribute a fighter's output to their opponent.

```sql
select count(*) filter (where fr.fighter_a_id = f.fighter_a_id) same_order,
       count(*) filter (where fr.fighter_a_id = f.fighter_b_id) swapped,
       count(*) filter (where fr.fighter_a_id is null) null_a
from fight_rounds fr join fights f on f.id=fr.fight_id;
```

**20,905 same order, 0 swapped, 0 null.** Clean.

**Round clock is reconstructible.** 8,904 of 8,944 fought fights carry an
`end_time` matching `^[0-9]+:[0-9]{2}$`; 40 are null; **none are malformed**.
So real round minutes are computable:

```sql
case when round_number < end_round then 5.0
     when round_number = end_round
       then least(5.0, split_part(end_time,':',1)::numeric
                     + split_part(end_time,':',2)::numeric/60.0)
end
```

Every per-minute figure in this audit uses that expression. Career per-minute
fields on the `fighters` table were used **nowhere**.

### Null and zero rates

Control seconds are populated from 2000 onward — the rounds where both corners
read 0 are genuine striking rounds, and the share rises from ~4% (2010) to ~12%
(2024), which is the direction the sport moved. Distance/clinch/ground and
takedown-attempt columns are **0% null** across the whole table. Significant
strikes attempted is null-or-zero in under 0.5% of rounds after 2005.

### The sample-size constraint that governs everything

```sql
-- UFC fights per fighter, all time
fighters 2,771 | median 4 | mean 6.46
1 fight: 439 | 2-4: 1,053 | 5-9: 644 | 10+: 635
```

**The median UFC fighter has four fights in this database.** Any metric that
needs a stable per-fighter estimate has to survive n=4, which is the reason
shrinkage is not optional anywhere in the spec.

Looking forward rather than back, for fights since 2010 (15,358 fighter-fight
rows), counting only fights **strictly before** each fight:

| support | share of fighter-fights |
|---|---|
| fighter has 3+ prior UFC fights | 63.9% |
| opponent has 3+ prior | 63.9% |
| **both have 3+ prior** | **48.1%** |
| both have 5+ prior | 31.8% |

So a metric requiring three prior fights on both sides is computable for
roughly **half** the card, and one requiring five for **under a third**.

## 2. The tests that decide the build order

### T-3. Does opponent adjustment improve striking? — **yes**

The question is not "is it sophisticated" but "does adjusting a fighter's prior
output for the opponents who produced it predict their **next** fight's output
better than the raw number". That is a falsifiable, out-of-sample test and it is
the whole case for the feature.

Construction, all strictly point-in-time:

- `raw_prior` = fighter's significant strikes landed per minute across all
  fights **before** this one.
- `opp_allow` = the opponent's significant strikes **absorbed** per minute
  across all of *their* fights **before** this one.
- `adj_prior` = `raw_prior × (opp_allow / league_absorbed_per_minute)` — what
  this fighter's rate implies against *this* opponent.
- `actual` = what the fighter actually landed per minute **in this fight**.

Filters: both fighters have 3+ prior fights, fight from 2010 on, at least one
minute fought. n = 6,902 fighter-fights.

| predictor | correlation with actual output |
|---|---|
| raw prior output | 0.2926 |
| opponent allowance alone | 0.2770 |
| **opponent-adjusted prior** | **0.3779** |

The adjusted figure beats **both** inputs, so it is combining information
rather than inheriting it from one side. The gain is +0.085 correlation, about
29% relative.

It also holds in every subgroup, which is what separates a result from a
coincidence:

| era | fight length | n | raw | opp only | **adjusted** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2010–2017 | ≥10 min | 1,526 | 0.2979 | 0.2965 | **0.4125** |
| 2010–2017 | <10 min | 950 | 0.2056 | 0.2058 | **0.2784** |
| 2018–2026 | ≥10 min | 2,822 | 0.3636 | 0.3108 | **0.4705** |
| 2018–2026 | <10 min | 1,604 | 0.1971 | 0.1990 | **0.2767** |
| all | all | 6,902 | 0.2926 | 0.2770 | **0.3779** |

Adjusted wins in all four cells, by +0.07 to +0.11. The signal is strongest in
long fights in the modern era — 0.36 → 0.47 — which is also where a researcher
most wants it.

### T-4. Does opponent adjustment improve wrestling? — **no, not as specified**

The same machinery, applied to takedowns landed per 15 minutes, adjusting by
the opponent's historical takedown defence:

| predictor | correlation |
|---|---|
| raw prior takedown rate | 0.3493 |
| opponent's takedown-defence rate alone | 0.1154 |
| opponent-adjusted (multiplicative) | **0.3466** |

**Adjustment made it slightly worse.** A second formulation, adjusting by
takedowns the opponent concedes *per 15 minutes* rather than as a success rate,
also lost to raw (0.3318 vs 0.3377). Only an additive combination gained
anything at all, and only a little (0.3600 vs 0.3377, +0.022 — a quarter of the
striking gain).

Two readings, both worth carrying:

1. A fighter's own takedown volume is already the dominant signal (0.35 raw,
   *higher* than striking's raw 0.29). Wrestling is more self-determined.
2. Takedown defence percentage is a weak predictor (0.115) — consistent with the
   owner's own instinct that "82% TDD" is a number begging for context, but the
   fix is not to fold it into an adjusted composite.

**Recommendation: do not ship an opponent-adjusted wrestling composite.** Show
the two numbers side by side — his takedown volume, and what that opponent has
historically conceded — and let them stay separate, because the data says they
do not combine cleanly.

### T-5. Is round-by-round decline a fighter trait? — **not as a percentage**

Split-half reliability: for each fighter, split their eligible fights (those
reaching round 3) into odd and even by date, compute the metric in each half,
and correlate the halves across fighters. If a metric cannot reproduce itself
on the same fighter, it is describing fights, not fighters.

| metric | n fighters | split-half reliability |
|---|---|---|
| R1 output level (4+ fights) | 952 | 0.3669 |
| R1 output level (6+ fights) | 602 | 0.4082 |
| **R3/R1 decline ratio (4+ fights)** | 952 | **0.0498** |
| **R3/R1 decline ratio (6+ fights)** | 602 | **0.0745** |

**The decline ratio has essentially zero reliability.** A fighter's measured
"drops 36% by round three" carries almost no information about what they will
do next time. Output *level* is a real trait; the *ratio* is not.

### T-6. …but the absolute drop is weakly real

Ratios divide by a small, noisy denominator — the same absolute drop yields a
wildly different percentage for a 2.0/min fighter and an 8.0/min fighter. So the
parameterisation was retested at 6+ fights (n=602):

| parameterisation | reliability |
|---|---|
| decline as **ratio** R3/R1 | 0.0745 |
| decline as **absolute drop** R1 − R3 (strikes/min) | **0.3032** |
| decline as log ratio | 0.2739 |
| decline in **attempts** R1 − R3 | 0.2599 |
| output **level** (R1+R3)/2 | **0.5587** |

The finding is precise and it is actionable: **the metric is not worthless, the
percentage is.** Expressed as an absolute drop in strikes per minute, late-round
decline has reliability 0.30 — modest, real, and requiring heavy shrinkage,
since roughly 70% of the observed between-fighter spread at n=6 is still noise.

**This indicts a shipped surface.** `v_fighter_consistency` builds its
`tireless / steady / tapers / fades / collapses` tiers on exactly the R3+/R1
**ratio** this test finds unreliable, and `fighter.html` renders those tiers.
`CLAUDE.md` already records that cardio does not predict winners; this is a
narrower and harder point — the tier does not reliably predict **the fighter's
own next cardio performance**. That is a published claim resting on a
measurement that does not reproduce, which makes changing it an owner call.

### T-7. Market history — two different assets, often confused

```sql
select case when captured_at < '2000-01-01' then 'epoch-sentinel' else 'real' end kind,
  count(*) rows, count(distinct fight_id) fights, count(distinct book_id) books
from fight_odds o join fights f on f.id=o.fight_id join events e on e.id=f.event_id group by 1;
```

| kind | rows | fights | books | event range |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| epoch-sentinel (`captured_at` = 1970-01-01) | 30,724 | **7,681** | 1 | 2007 → 2026 |
| real capture | 84,864 | **231** | 13 | 2026-05-30 → 2026-11-14 |

26.6% of `fight_odds` rows carry a Unix-epoch timestamp. **This is not
corruption** — it is a historical single-book odds backfill with no capture time
to record, and it is what gives Factor Lab its market-control cohort across
7,681 fights.

The consequence for this project is a hard boundary:

- **Historical research filtered on market price: feasible** — 7,681 fights.
- **Anything needing capture time, movement, or multi-book spread: 231 fights,
  since 2026-05-30.** Under four months deep.

So "similar matchups, filtered by how the market priced them" is buildable now.
"Similar matchups, filtered by how the market *moved*" is not, and will not be
for a year. A timestamp of 1970 must never be rendered or differenced.

## 3. Leakage — how each test avoided it

Every predictive figure above was computed with a window frame that ends one
row **before** the row being scored:

```sql
sum(sl) over (partition by fid order by event_date, fight_id
              rows between unbounded preceding and 1 preceding)
```

The opponent's baseline is computed the same way, on the opponent's own history
strictly before the shared fight date. The scored outcome — what the fighter did
*in* that fight — never enters either predictor.

The `fighters` table's career aggregates (`slpm`, `sapm`, `td_avg`, `td_acc`,
`td_def`, `str_def`, `wins`, `losses`) are **current-state snapshots** that
already contain the fight being predicted. They were deliberately used in none
of these tests, and they must not be used in historical validation. This is the
same defect that once inflated the Factor Lab record factor to 75.5%.

## 4. What was not tested, and should be before it ships

- **Shrinkage constants.** Reliability was measured; the shrinkage weight that
  should follow from it was not fitted. Needed before any per-fighter number
  is rendered.
- **Division and era normalisation.** Every test pooled all weight classes.
  Flyweight and heavyweight pace differ materially, and the league mean is
  drifting (average significant strikes per round rose from 24 in 2010 to 37 in
  2023).
- **Second-order opponent adjustment.** The opponent's allowance is itself
  unadjusted for who *they* faced. One iteration is likely enough; it is untested.
- **Control time and the clinch/ground split**, which are populated and were not
  exercised by any test here.
